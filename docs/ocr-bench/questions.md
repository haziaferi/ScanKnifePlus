# Open OCR questions: answers from the 6T

Eight design questions for OCR, each scored on the OnePlus 6T (Tesseract4Android 4.11.0, openmp, LSTM) on 2026-10-23. Full tables, per set and per model: [questions-results.md](questions-results.md), generated by `questions.py score`. How the sets are made and run: README, "Open questions".

## Pages and how they were scored

| Set | Pages | Truth | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| clean | 5 | corpus truth | the 5 corpus pages rendered from `corpus.pdf` at 600 DPI |
| photo | 5 | corpus truth | the printed corpus photographed (6T, about 370-390 DPI), warped with the hand-marked quad |
| ita | 29 x 2 | the page's text layer | the user's Italian PDF of food tables, simulated scans at 200 and 300 ppi |
| eng | 24 x 2 | the page's text layer | the four English PDFs of PaperKnife+ PC's study (manuals, a brochure), simulated scans at 200 and 300 ppi |
| shots | 9 | none | receipt, ticket, sachet and six A4 pages from the scanner benchmark, hand-marked quads |
| books | 9 | none | the textbook photos of the field test, hand-marked quads |
| hand | 2 | none | the two handwritten sheets |

A simulated scan follows that study's `ocr_measure.py`: the page rendered at the scan resolution, saved as JPEG q85, put back on an image-only page and rendered again at the target resolution. Photos are warped at their natural size with cv2 and then resized (area filter down, bilinear up), in BT.601 grey. Every image is read by `imagesFast`/`imagesBest` exactly as prepared, with PSM 3 unless stated and `user_defined_dpi` set to the rule's DPI. Fixed DPI needs the physical page size: A4 for the corpus and the A4 shots, the PDF page size, and estimates for books (field.tsv) and the receipt, ticket and sachet (190, 120, 180 mm long edge); the app itself would only know a size if it assumed one.

Metrics: CER and WER against the truth (reading order counts), **missed words** (truth words absent from the output, order-free, the main accuracy measure here because table and two-column pages make CER mostly a reading-order score), and for the PDFs also the study's own position-paired **found** and **junk** (`ocr_score.py`, threshold 0). Pages without truth: words read and mean word confidence. Time is Tesseract's recognition alone; memory is native heap growth and process PSS, worst page.

## 1. Resolution: fixed DPI or x-height rule

**Method.** Every page at 150, 200, 250, 300 and 400 DPI and scaled so its estimated x-height is 20, 25 or 30 px. The x-height is estimated before OCR from the grey page (`xheight()`: 3x3 median, adaptive threshold, connected components 6-200 px tall that have a same-height neighbour on the same baseline, mode of their heights; 100-300 ms per page on the PC). Fast for every image; best for clean and photo at every rule and for the other sets at dpi200, dpi300 and xh25 only, to keep the device time down.

**Reproducing the study first** (Italian file, fast, both scan resolutions, its own "found" metric): 150 DPI found 71.1% (study: 72.4%), 200 DPI 86.4% (study: 86.1%), 300 DPI 78.9%. The study's result holds on the phone with tessdata_fast.

| Rule (fast) | clean missed | photo missed | ita missed (found) | eng missed (found) | Mean over the 4 sets | s/page ita / eng |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dpi150 | 0.86 | 1.71 | 28.1 (71.1) | 5.56 (94.5) | 9.06 | 2.5 / 2.1 |
| **dpi200** | 1.35 | 1.10 | **12.6 (86.4)** | 4.80 (95.1) | **4.96** | 3.7 / 2.4 |
| dpi250 | 0.86 | 1.22 | 15.9 (82.5) | 4.61 (95.3) | 5.66 | 3.7 / 2.6 |
| dpi300 | 1.10 | 0.61 | 19.6 (78.9) | 4.49 (95.4) | 6.45 | 4.1 / 2.9 |
| dpi400 | 1.35 | 1.35 | 13.0 (85.5) | 4.66 (95.2) | 5.08 | 5.5 / 3.6 |
| xh20 | 1.22 | 1.22 | 15.1 (83.5) | 4.59 (95.3) | 5.53 | 3.9 / 2.8 |
| xh25 | 0.98 | 1.47 | 19.6 (78.9) | 4.56 (95.4) | 6.66 | 4.0 / 3.5 |
| xh30 | 0.98 | 1.96 | 19.0 (79.5) | 4.82 (95.0) | 6.68 | 4.6 / 3.9 |

Per set: clean, photo, eng and books barely move between 200 and 400 DPI or any x-height (missed words within about 1 point; books' mean confidence 91.4-93.0). The shots read about 3% fewer words at 150-200 DPI (3,058-3,084) than at 250 DPI and up or any x-height rule (3,157-3,188), at the same mean confidence (91-93): the small print on the ticket and sachet is the likely loss, and there is no truth to confirm it. Fixed 150 DPI is the only rule that fails broadly: 54 of 58 Italian pages lose 5+ points against their best rule and an English figure page loses 46% of its words. Per-set winners (fewest missed words): clean dpi150 (by 0.1-0.5 points, noise), photo dpi300, ita dpi200, eng dpi300 (within 0.3 of everything from 200 up), shots xh20 and books dpi300 by mean confidence (no truth, differences under 1 point).

**Where it fails.** Only the Italian tables separate the rules, and not by x-height:

| Rule | 200 ppi scans: missed / est. x-height px | 300 ppi scans: missed / est. x-height px |
|---|---|---|
| dpi200 | 12.7 / 17 | 12.5 / 17 |
| dpi300 | 15.4 / 26 | 23.9 / 25 |
| dpi400 | 13.6 / 34 | 12.4 / 33 |
| xh25 | 15.5 / 25 | 23.8 / 25 |
| xh30 | 15.8 / 30 | 22.1 / 30 |

At the same x-height (25 px) the 300 ppi scans read at their own resolution lose 24% of the words, the 200 ppi scans enlarged to it 15%; 17 px and 33 px are both fine. On the bad pages whole table blocks are missing from the output (one page: 191 words at 300 DPI against 305 at 200 and 306 at 400), so it is Tesseract's page layout analysis dropping regions, which flips with small changes of scale, not text that is "too large". Question 3 shows PSM 6 (no block analysis) recovers most of them.

**The x-height estimate** matched Tesseract's own (median box height of words made only of x-height letters) within 15% on 55 of 56 measurable pages outside the Italian file (median ratio 0.90-0.98 per set), but read 26% high on the Italian tables (31 of 34 pages), so the x-height rules ran those pages smaller than intended.

**Answer: fixed 200 DPI of the assumed page size, tessdata_fast.** It has the fewest missed words over the four sets with truth (4.96% mean), wins the Italian file by a wide margin, stays within about a point of the best rule on every other set with truth, and is the second cheapest (3.7 s per Italian page against 4.1 s at 300 and 5.5 s at 400; native heap +23-27 MB against +39-44 at 300). The user's hypothesis does not hold on this data: an x-height rule did not beat fixed DPI on any set with truth, the corpus and English pages are insensitive to resolution from 200 DPI up, and the Italian losses are a layout effect at particular scales rather than a function of x-height. 400 DPI is the safer second choice (5.08%, no set worse than 13%) at about 1.5x the time. Evidence is thin where it matters: one Italian file decides the ranking between 200, 300 and 400, the corpus has 10 pages and 8 pt text is only one of them (fine at every rule), and nothing here was smaller than 8 pt.

## 2. Model at the winning rule

| dpi200 | clean missed | photo missed | ita missed (found) | eng missed | books mean conf | s/page ita / books |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fast | 1.35 | 1.10 | 12.6 (86.4) | 4.80 | 92.6 | 3.7 / 3.4 |
| best | 1.59 | 1.35 | 16.3 (87.5) | 4.84 | 93.2 | 11.7 / 11.8 |

**Answer: tessdata_fast.** best is never better by more than noise (on the Italian file it finds 1 point more by position but misses 3.6 points more by word bag), and costs 2.4-4x the time (11.7 s per Italian page) and 8-14 MB more native heap. Same conclusion as the corpus, field and scanner-bench runs.

## 3. Page layout (PSM) at dpi200, fast

| PSM | clean CER / missed | photo CER / missed | ita CER / missed (found) | eng CER / missed | s/page ita |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 auto | 5.32 / 1.35 | 8.64 / 1.10 | 55.3 / 12.6 (86.4) | 10.6 / 4.80 | 3.7 |
| 1 auto + OSD | 5.32 / 1.35 | 8.64 / 1.10 | 55.3 / 12.6 (86.4) | 10.6 / 4.80 | 4.3 |
| 4 single column | 5.83 / 0.73 | 6.01 / 0.98 | 3.7 / 13.3 (85.7) | 11.0 / 4.83 | 3.5 |
| 6 single block | 5.75 / 0.24 | 11.38 / 0.98 | **2.1 / 5.9 (92.6)** | 12.2 / 4.82 | 3.2 |
| 11 sparse text | 6.34 / 1.35 | 7.21 / 0.86 | 5.8 / 12.3 (86.8) | 13.1 / 4.82 | 3.2 |

PSM 1 gives text identical to 3 and costs 0.6-1.6 s and about 35 MB more. On the Italian tables PSM 3 reads cell by cell in block order (CER 55%) and drops whole blocks; 4, 6 and 11 read the rows in order (CER 2-6%), and 6 also keeps the blocks 3 drops (missed 5.9% against 12.6%). On the corpus photos 4 and 11 fix the receipt's columns (CER 2.0 and 3.8 against 31.3) while 6 splits lines on the footnote and two-column pages; the two-column page is wrong in every mode (34-47% CER, nothing missed). On the English manuals PSM 3 has the lowest CER and every mode misses the same words.

**Answer: keep PSM 3 as the default and drop any idea of PSM 1.** No single mode wins: 6 is far better on tables, 4 on receipts and single-column pages, 3 on mixed layouts and multi-column books (the earlier layout runs: 4 and 6 merge columns line by line). Offering a "table / receipt" reading (PSM 6 or 4) is a product decision; the data says it would turn the Italian file from 87% to 93% of words found and its text from unusable order into rows. Not tested: books with 4/6 (no truth; known to merge columns), and whether PSM 6 also removes the scale sensitivity of question 1.

## 4. Word-confidence filter for the .txt (dpi200)

| Threshold (fast) | ita dropped / real | eng dropped / real | photo dropped / real | shots dropped % | books dropped % | handwriting dropped % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 51 / 29 | 199 / 15 | 2 / 0 | 2.4 | 1.3 | 36 |
| 40 | 109 / 62 | 279 / 20 | 3 / 0 | 3.4 | 1.9 | 48 |
| 50 | 189 / 109 | 348 / 35 | 4 / 0 | 4.3 | 2.5 | 59 |
| 60 | 283 / 176 | 426 / 55 | 7 / 2 | 4.9 | 3.2 | 70 |
| 80 | 723 / 548 | 646 / 126 | 15 / 8 | 7.7 | 6.0 | 85 |

"real" = dropped words that are in the page's truth. The junk the filter removes sits on the English pages' figures and screenshots (at 40: 259 junk words for 20 real ones); on the Italian tables low confidence is mostly real words (62 of 109 at 40). Each 10 points from 40 to 50 costs about 42% real words, from 50 to 60 about 50%. CER moves by at most about half a point up to 60 and rises from 80.

**Answer: 40 for the .txt (fast), the PDF keeps every word.** It removes most of the junk (306 words) for 82 real ones out of about 31,000 truth words, and changes nothing on the corpus. Above 50 the filter mostly removes real words; on handwriting every threshold removes a large share, which is question 7's signal, not a reason to filter.

## 5. Rotation: OSD confidence floor

`imagesOsd`: every upright page at dpi200 turned 0/90/180/270, `detectOrientationScript()` on each (fast data's osd, 0.78 s median per call). 50 pages, 200 calls: clean 5, photo 5, Italian 29 (300 ppi scans), books 9, handwriting 2.

| Floor | Correct | Wrong turn | Left turned | Upright page turned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 193 | 3 | 4 | 1 |
| 1 | 189 | 1 | 10 | 1 |
| **2** | **188** | **0** | **12** | **0** |
| 3 | 186 | 0 | 14 | 0 |
| 5 | 180 | 0 | 20 | 0 |
| 10 | 170 | 0 | 30 | 0 |
| 15 | 95 | 0 | 105 | 0 |

Every wrong answer had confidence 0.3-1.5 (three on handwriting, one on a corpus photo); right answers on print were 2.9-23 (clean median 18.7, Italian 14.7, books 11.1, photos 10.6, with one photo at 0.2). At floor 2 the 12 pages left turned are 4 handwriting turns and 8 printed ones (5 corpus photo, 3 book). The field test's suggestion of 5 leaves 8 more pages turned for no fewer errors here.

**Answer: floor 2** (turn when confidence >= 2): no wrong turns in 200 calls, 94% corrected. Thin evidence below 3: the wrong answers were all under 1.5 but only four occurred, so 3 is a reasonable margin if a wrong turn is judged worse than a missed one (186 correct).

## 6. Confirmed crop against the automatic crop (dpi200)

The labelled quad against the current one-shot detector's quad (`old` in the scanner benchmark's detect-ours.json), through the same cv2 warp at the label crop's scale. The detector's corners were rotated to match the label's first corner, so orientation (question 5) plays no part.

| | Pages | Detector IoU >= 0.9 | Label text's words lost in the auto crop: median | Pages losing > 10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| corpus photos | 5 | 5 | 3.9% (footnotes page 8.6%) | 0 of 5 |
| shots | 9 | 5 | 22% | 7 of 9 |
| books and notes | 11 | 3 | 70% | 11 of 11 |
| all (fast) | 25 | 13 | 40% | 18 of 25 |

On the corpus the automatic crop reads nearly as well as the label (within 3 points of missed words except the 8 pt footnotes page, 8.6% against 1.2%; the receipt even reads in line order from the auto crop, CER 0.2% against 31% with plain grey, a side effect of the slightly different margins). Elsewhere it loses words even at high IoU: a page-held-by-hand at IoU 0.93 loses 22%, the ticket at 0.95 loses 41%, the receipt at 0.94 20%; two quads skewed across the page (IoU 0.74-0.76) lose 99%; every book quad that picked a column or a block loses 55-100%.

**Answer: OCR must run on a crop the user has confirmed** (the crop screen), not on the one-shot detection. Without confirmation, 18 of 25 pages lose more than 10% of their words. This scores today's detector; the port in wt/p24 should be re-scored with `prepare --set q6` and its quads.

## 7. Handwriting flag

Mean word confidence of every reading (8 rules x fast/best, 1,524 printed readings, 22 handwritten):

| | Handwritten | Printed |
|---|---|---|
| Mean word confidence | 34.6-50.8 | 57.6-96.6 |
| Share of words under 50 | 45.7-72.1% | 0.0-46.2% |

Below 55: 0 printed readings flagged, 0 handwritten missed; below 60: 3 printed readings flagged (two English manual pages with screenshots and figures); below 50: one handwritten reading missed. The share of words under 50 overlaps (one printed page at 46%).

**Answer: flag the page as "looks handwritten, recognition will be poor" when its mean word confidence is below 55.** It separated every reading here, with a 7-point margin to the lowest printed reading. Evidence is thin: only two handwritten sheets, both in block capitals; cursive or a single handwritten line on a printed form is untested.

## 8. OCR input image (dpi200)

| Input | photo CER / missed (fast) | photo CER / missed (best) | corpus receipt CER (fast) | shots words / conf | books words / conf |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| plain grey | 8.64 / 1.10 | 8.64 / 1.35 | 31.3 | 3084 / 91.5 | 4369 / 92.6 |
| Grayscale filter | **5.91 / 0.73** | 5.91 / 1.10 | **1.1** | 3078 / 91.8 | 4329 / 92.7 |
| stored PDF page | 5.97 / 1.10 | 5.91 / 1.10 | 1.4 | 3043 / 92.2 | 4295 / 92.5 |

"stored PDF page" = the page as the PDF holds it with the default Original filter (2400 px, JPEG q85), grey and scaled to dpi200. The receipt result of #56 holds at 200 DPI: plain grey reads the receipt as two columns (31% CER, all words present), the auto-levelled Grayscale keeps each line with its price (1.1%). The stored page does nearly as well, because by 200 DPI it is downscaled from 2400 px anyway. Books, shots and notes do not move.

**Answer: feed Tesseract our Grayscale filter of the full-resolution warp** (contrast-stretched grey), whatever filter the user picked for the PDF. Reading the stored 2400 px page instead would cost almost nothing at 200 DPI and avoids a second decode of the photo; that trade (one less full-resolution pass against about 0.4 points of missed words on the corpus) is a design choice.

## What this cost and what was not tested

- Device: 96 instrumentations, about 5.8 hours of device-test runs including builds and installs, 168 min of recognition and 2.5 min of OSD. Best runs and long chunks were killed by the phone's low-memory killer several times; the driver re-ran the missing images.
- Not tested: tessdata_best for every rule on the PDF, shots and book sets (only dpi200, dpi300, xh25); PSM modes on books and shots (no truth); text smaller than 8 pt; any page at an angle or with the curl of a real book page against a truth; cursive handwriting; the new detector from wt/p24.
- One Italian file decides question 1's ranking and most of question 3; the corpus is 5 pages in two renderings.
