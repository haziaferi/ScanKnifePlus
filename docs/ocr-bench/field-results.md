# OCR benchmark: field photos

Generated by `docs/ocr-bench/score.py --field`; method in `docs/ocr-bench/README.md` ("Field mode"). Real photos without ground truth: the numbers are detection, orientation, cost, confidence and agreement between configurations, not accuracy. The photos and their recognised text stay out of git (`docs/ocr-bench/field-out/`, git-ignored).

## Set `6t-field`

Labels come from the manifest (`field.tsv`). The physical long edge of each OCR input sets the target DPI; it is an estimate (the books were not measured), so DPI values are approximate.

### Page detection and orientation

Detection = the scanner's one-shot detection on the full-resolution photo; quad % = its share of the photo area; covers = what the quad actually covers, judged from the preview. OCR input = what was recognised: `quad` (the warped detection) or `frame` (the whole photo, where the quad was the wrong region), with the assumed physical long edge of that input. OSD = Tesseract orientation detection on the grey page at 300 DPI: the page is turned clockwise by `rotate` before recognition; confidence above about 15 is reasonably sure (Tesseract's guide); 400 DPI shown when it differs.

### Agreement between configurations

A proxy for accuracy without ground truth: CER / WER / unshared words (%) of one output against another of the same photo, primary language. Fast vs best takes best as the reference; 300 vs 400 takes 400. Unshared words = the reference's words missing from the other output regardless of order, so it separates recognition from reading order. Low disagreement does not prove the text right (both can make the same mistake), high disagreement shows at least one of them is wrong.
