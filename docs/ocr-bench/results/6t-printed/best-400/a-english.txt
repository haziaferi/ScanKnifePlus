The Reading Room

The reading room opens at half past eight, and by nine most of the long oak tables are taken. Students
arrive with laptops, retired engineers with folders of yellowed drawings, and a few visitors who only

want to see the painted ceiling. The rules are simple: no food, no loud calls, and every book that leaves
the shelves must be signed out at the front desk.

Behind the desk, two librarians keep a ledger that has been in use since 1952. Each entry records the
title, the shelf mark, the time of loan and the initials of the reader. Over seventy years the handwriting

changes from fountain pen to ballpoint, and the shelf marks change from Roman numerals to a
decimal scheme introduced in 1987.

Last spring the library began to scan the ledger. A volunteer photographs each page under a pair of

daylight lamps, and a small program straightens the image, removes the shadow of the binding and

stores the result as a searchable document. The hardest pages are those where ink has faded to a pale

brown, or where a later reader added notes in pencil between the lines.
So far 4,316 pages have been processed, about 61 percent of the total. The volunteers estimate that the
remaining volumes will take another eighteen months, provided the lamps survive. Anyone who
wishes to help may ask at the desk; training takes about an hour, and no previous experience is needed.
Questions about the project, the opening hours (Monday to Saturday, 8:30 to 19:00) or access for
wheelchair users can be sent to the address printed on the

back of every library card.
