# Scanner benchmark: ScanKnife+ against OSS-DocumentScanner

A harness, not a feature: it scores ScanKnife+'s scanner against [OSS-DocumentScanner](https://github.com/ossappscollective/OSS-DocumentScanner) (MIT, Copyright (c) 2022 Martin Guillon) at commit `ff05fc52b601d1cccb4076f3f6c3318f74e72f0c`, using ports of their real algorithms as the reference, on five things: outline detection, the book gutter, colour filters, OCR and auto-capture. Nothing here ships: the Python ports and the Kotlin test double exist only to measure, and no app or scanner-core main code changes.

Results: [results.md](results.md) (PC side) and [ocr-results.md](ocr-results.md) (after the device run).

Their sources are fetched for reading only (`gh api repos/ossappscollective/OSS-DocumentScanner/contents/<path>?ref=<sha>`) and are not in this repository.

## Photos and labels

The user's photos are never committed or uploaded; every derived image, crop, label and per-photo output stays in the work folder (`WORK`) outside the repository. The photo folder holds `books/` (nine open-book photos `book-01..09` and two handwritten sheets `hand-01..02`, with `field.tsv` from the OCR field mode), `corpus/` (the five printed OCR corpus pages `a-english..e-layout`, upright, ground truth in `docs/ocr-bench/corpus/truth/`) and `shots/camera/` (11 stock-camera shots) plus `shots/glasscam/` (2 GlassCam shots). EXIF orientation is applied everywhere: `prepare` writes upright PNGs for the JVM, and Python reads through `cv2.imread`, which applies it.

The answer key is `labels.json`, marked by hand on the upright photos and never derived from either detector: `{"<set>/<file>": {"quad": [[x, y] x4: TL, TR, BR, BL], "fold": [[x, y], [x, y]] or null, "note": str}}`. `prepare` matches keys to photos by file name. Corners are given in reading order, so a warp with a label comes out upright. Six labels cannot score an outline detector and are reported apart (`UNSUITABLE` in bench.py): book-06..09 (the page or photocopy fills the frame, so the label is the frame), the folded page (two planes) and the bottle-shaped sachet; book-01..05 and the two notes are cut off by the frame (corners on its edge) and are scored; the e-layout page's bottom-right corner is a curled tip.

## Method

**1. Outline detection.** Each photo is detected three ways at full resolution, upright:
- *Ours*: `DocumentDetector.detectFromRgba`, the one-shot path (`OcrBenchmarkTest` uses the same), after `StoredImage.flattenOntoWhite`.
- *Theirs*: their app's one-shot path. `processCameraImage` calls `getJSONDocumentCornersFromFile(imagePath, { resizeThreshold: previewResizeThreshold * 1.5, areaScaleMinFactor })` (app/utils/ui/index.common.ts:1874) with `PREVIEW_RESIZE_THRESHOLD = 200` and `AREA_SCALE_MIN_FACTOR = 0.1` (app/utils/constants.ts:72-73). Android decodes the photo to a long edge of 300 and turns it upright; with `shrunkImageHeight` 0 the detector does not resize again and sets `medianBlurValue = 3` (plugin-nativeprocessor/platforms/android/cpp/document_detector.cpp:194-202); everything else is the `DetectOptions` default in DocumentDetector.h, which leaves the Hough refinement off (`houghLinesThreshold = 0`). The port then follows scanPoint exactly: 10 px black border, median blur, per channel of the RGBA bitmap (B, G, R) a threshold-160 mask and Canny at 120/240 down to 20/40, findSquares on each (contour area 10%-92% of the frame, approxPolyDP at 2% of the arc, convex, 10 px from the border, max |cos| < 0.4), stopping early once the best has |cos| < 0.3 and area > 20%; the result is the best `area + weight * (1 - maxCos)` with weight 3,000,000 minus one per mask, sorted by `sortPoints`.
- *Hybrid*: our detector's candidate pool (all three threshold masks of `detectQuadFromGrayscale`, before our clustering) ranked by their sort factor, with their per-mask weights and the area converted to their 300 px scale. Since the weight term dominates, this picks the most rectangular candidate.

Scores per photo: IoU of the detected quad with the label (>= 0.9 counts as correct, no quad counts as 0) and the largest corner error under the best one-to-one corner matching, as % of the label's longer diagonal. Time is PC time for detection on the decoded photo (decode excluded, their downscale included), Python/OpenCV for theirs and the JVM for ours; it is not device time.

**2. Gutter.** Each two-page spread (a marked fold in the middle half of the labelled quad; a fold near an edge marks one page with the facing page's edge and is skipped) is warped with its labelled quad (cv2, natural size) and turned so the fold is vertical (detectGutterAndSplit only looks for vertical folds; the app's quarter turns would do the same). Their `detectGutterAndSplit` is called nowhere in their app; it runs as in their desktop test app (cpp/cpp_test_app/scanner.cpp:159, 780-782): long edge 300 with a 10 px black border, `minPageWidthRatio` 0.4. It is compared with our fixed split at 0.5 (`BookSplit.DEFAULT_SPLIT`). Error = |predicted - marked fold| as % of the spread's width, the marked fold being the mean x of the two fold points after the warp.

**3. Colour filters.** Every labelled photo is warped with its label by our `PerspectiveCrop` (corpus pages to A4 at 300 DPI, 2480 x 3508; the rest at natural size capped at 3508 px), so detection plays no part. Our six `DocumentFilters` run on the JVM, their four transforms in Python with the options their app uses: the transform list offers `enhance`, `whitepaper` and `color` with no options (app/utils/localized_constant.ts:3-7), so every value is the C++ default; `whitepaper2` is not offered in the app and is included because it exists. Metrics on CIELAB L* (0-100): paper = pixels above the unfiltered page's Otsu threshold, eroded 5 x 5, ink = the rest, not eroded (text strokes are only a few pixels wide), so every filter is judged on the same pixels; uniformity = standard deviation and p5-p95 spread of paper L*; contrast = median paper L* minus median ink L*, and stroke-core contrast = median paper L* minus the 10th percentile of ink L* (it still sees text a filter has thinned). Medians over pages. These measure flat paper and dark ink, not legibility: a filter that wipes out faint strokes can still score well, which step 4 checks.

**4. OCR.** `bench.py score` writes three image sets (git-ignored, in `WORK/ocr/`): `sb-corpus` and `sb-books` hold every filter of every corpus page, book photo and handwritten sheet in grey (the labels give the corners in reading order, so every warp is upright); `sb-oss` holds their OCR preprocessing of the unfiltered page: `ossocr-app` is what their app sends (app/services/ocr.ts:264-275 passes `detectContours: 0`, so the whole page goes through adaptive Gaussian threshold 339/47 and inversion, with a 15 px white border), `ossocr-blocks` the header defaults (text blocks from a 70 x 4 close, 40 x 40 dilation and external contours of more than 200 points, cropped, sorted by their `getYSortValue` key, each with a 15 px white border). `images.tsv` lists page, variant, language, DPI (300: their default `dpi`, and the corpus warps' true resolution) and block order. On the phone each image is recognised as it is (`imagesFast`, `imagesBest`): PSM 3 (their `PSM_AUTO` default), LSTM (their `OEM_DEFAULT` on LSTM-only traineddata), `user_defined_dpi` from the manifest; besides the text and words it writes lines.tsv, the text-line level their app filters at (`OCR_ITERATOR_LEVEL = 2`, app/utils/constants.ts:109). `score.py --images` stitches the blocks of a page in manifest order and scores the text against the corpus truth where the page has one (CER, WER, missed words), word counts and mean confidence otherwise; for their pipeline it also scores the text after their line filter (`oss_ocr_filter.py`). Their app defaults to tessdata_best (app/services/ocr.ts:152).

**5. Auto-capture.** Pure JVM, seeded, deterministic. `AutoScanHandlerDouble` ports their handler's logic with its defaults (distance 50 px between consecutive frames' corners, pre-delay 1000 ms, then a 101 x 10 ms progress job, per-quad hashing with `31 * x + y` point hashes and the hash bimap; a quad that stays matched after a capture never re-triggers). Our `AutoCaptureDetector` runs with its real defaults on a virtual `MicrosClock`. Both get the same synthetic sequences at 30 fps for 6 s, 20 runs each (scenarios in results.md): ours in normalised coordinates, theirs in pixels of a 480 x 640 analysis frame (CameraX's default 640 x 480 analysis size; the threshold is in pixels, so 960 x 1280 is shown too), quantised as their 200 px live detection quantises corners.

## Running

From the worktree root, with an interpreter that has OpenCV and numpy:

    PYTHON=/path/to/python bash docs/scanner-bench/run.sh PHOTOS LABELS WORK

`oss-detect` and the filter metrics resume from their JSON in `WORK` (delete it to recompute). The OCR device step, phone connected and the app not installed:

    bash docs/ocr-bench/run.sh --images sb-corpus=WORK/ocr/sb-corpus --images sb-oss=WORK/ocr/sb-oss
    IMAGES_METHODS=imagesFast bash docs/ocr-bench/run.sh --images sb-books=WORK/ocr/sb-books

## Where a port may not be faithful

- OpenCV 5.0.0 (Python) instead of their 4.13: no API change was needed; `findContours`, `approxPolyDP`, `Canny`, `detailEnhance`, `kmeans` and `adaptiveThreshold` behave the same as far as the bench can tell, but bit-exactness was not checked against their native build.
- Their one-shot input is an Android `BitmapFactory` downsample (power-of-two `inSampleSize`, then density scaling); the port uses `INTER_AREA` to the same size. Contour sorting ties may order differently (`std::sort` is not stable).
- `whitepaper`'s `colorBalance` searches a `cv::reduce`d histogram (one row with the pixel count), so both bounds are 0 and it returns the image unchanged; the port reproduces that, as it does `normalizeKernel`'s negative-scale quirk and `csWhitePer` 99.5 truncating to 99.
- `color` runs on the RGBA bitmap as if it were BGR; its HSV distance divides the integer hue difference by 180, so only S and V count. `kmeans` seeds from OpenCV's RNG, seeded here for repeatability.
- The auto-capture double runs its 1010 ms job exactly (the real dispatcher adds slack) and accepts at most one quad per frame (their detector returns one; with two, the original's remove-inside-forEach would throw on a standard ArrayList iterator).

## Findings (PC, 2026-10-23)

29 photos, all hand-marked; 6 labels cannot score an outline (a page or photocopy filling the frame, a folded page, a bottle-shaped sachet) and are reported apart. Details and per-photo rows in results.md.
- **Outline detection is close overall, with different failure modes.** On the 23 scorable photos: ours 14 correct (IoU >= 0.9), median IoU 0.92, median corner error 4.7%; theirs 15 correct, 0.94, 4.3%, but it returns nothing on 4 of them (a book back cover on a glossy table, a red cover, a white page on a light surface, a handwritten sheet), where ours always returns a quad. When theirs finds the page its corners are tighter (1-2% on most shots, vs 2-5% for ours). Ours goes wrong on book pages cut off by the frame (it picks a text column or figure block); theirs, whose added border lets a contour run along the frame edge, does better there, and also on the two pages on a patterned wood surface (0.96-0.99 vs 0.74). All five corpus pages are correct for both. PC time is similar (median 59 ms ours, 44 ms theirs at their 300 px working size).
- **Their ranking is not what makes the difference**: the hybrid (our candidates, their score) gets the same 14 correct, within 0.02 IoU of ours on most photos, and clearly worse on the five where ours was already wrong (it prefers the most rectangular candidate, often a text block). What differs is candidate generation (their per-channel threshold and Canny masks at 300 px vs our Sobel-Otsu masks at 700 px).
- **Gutter: the fixed middle beats detectGutterAndSplit** on the three real spreads: median error 1.2% (max 1.9%) for 0.5 against 21.8% (16.8-23.4%) for theirs, which locks onto low-texture columns (margins, figures) rather than the fold. Only three spreads, two of them photocopies whose fold is a dark band, so this is indicative only.
- **Colour filters**: their `whitepaper` and our Whiteboard both flatten the paper (paper L* std 2.0 and 0.8 against 5.4 unfiltered) and keep dark stroke cores (82-83 L*), but whitepaper thins the strokes (median ink contrast 12.5 vs 35.7) and `whitepaper2` whitens everything but the darkest stroke cores. `enhance` behaves like a milder version of our Auto (std 7.3, stroke contrast 69 vs 84) and is the slowest (about 2.2 s per page on the PC against 30 ms for Auto). `color` posterises the paper to one value (p5-p95 spread 0) but loses contrast (stroke cores 56, below unfiltered 58). Our Auto, Lighten and Grayscale raise contrast the most among the continuous-tone modes but keep the shading (std 7-8). Whether any of this helps recognition is step 4.
- **Auto-capture**: held steady, ours captures after 700 ms and theirs after about 2 s (1000 ms pre-delay plus the 1010 ms progress job). Ours compares consecutive frames at 2% of the frame per axis, theirs at 50 px (10% of a 480 px frame): ours loses captures to hand shake from 1% amplitude and fails at 2%, theirs survives up to 2% at 480 x 640 but not at 960 x 1280, where 50 px is half as much of the frame; and ours captures a page drifting at 15% of the frame per second, which theirs (and its longer wait) does not. Theirs restarts on any frame without a detection (5% random dropouts: 4 of 20 runs capture, ours 20 of 20). Theirs captured 4 of 20 runs while two documents alternated every 5 frames; this looks like its hash bimap, which is never cleared, mapping a recurring quantised quad to a job of an earlier appearance so the current one is never cancelled (not traced further).
